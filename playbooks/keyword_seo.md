# Playbook: Keyword Research & SEO Map

The keyword map is one asset with three consumers: the listing (indexing),
PPC (targeting), and rank strategy (which battles to fund). A diluted map
wastes PPC money AND flattens conversion, which kills organic rank — the
two failure modes compound. Be ruthless about relevance.

## Structure & the funding logic
- **head** (1–3 words, huge volume): 5–8 terms. You index on them day 1; you
  WIN them last (month 3+, if ever). They exist in the map so the title and
  exact campaigns are ready when rank arrives — not to spend on early.
- **mid** (3–5 words, qualified): 15–25 terms. The launch battleground —
  winnable in weeks, meaningful volume. ~70% of launch PPC goes here.
- **longtail** (5+ words / problem phrasing): the rest, per the genome's bias.
  Cheap clicks, 1.5–2× head-term CVR, first page-1 rankings. Early wins here
  feed the algorithm the conversion data that later unlocks mid terms.

## Intent tags (assign by imagining the searcher's screen)
- **buy**: transactional, product-shaped ("magnetic spice rack for refrigerator")
  → title, exact campaigns
- **compare**: alternatives/brands ("simplehuman spice rack alternative")
  → bullets, product-targeting campaigns; NEVER backend competitor names (policy)
- **problem**: no product named ("spices falling off fridge", "small kitchen
  no counter space") → longtail gold: bullets, description, backend, broad
- **browse**: category strolling → backend only; zero PPC budget

## Placement rules
- title: 2–4 highest-volume buy terms that read as ONE natural phrase —
  Amazon indexes the words; humans buy the sentence
- bullets: mid terms woven into benefit statements, one per bullet max
- backend: everything the visible copy can't carry — synonyms, common
  misspellings only if genuinely common, Spanish variants (condimento,
  especiero, refrigerador), use-context words (rv, camper, dorm, garage);
  never repeat title/bullet words, never brands, never ASINs
- ppc_exact: terms you intend to OWN (proven or must-win); ppc_broad:
  discovery feeders whose search-term reports refill this very map

## Relevance scoring (0–1) — the strict test
"Would a searcher of this exact phrase buy THIS product at THIS price?"
- 1.0: the phrase describes this product and its differentiator
- 0.7: right product family, unstated variant assumptions (size/color)
- 0.4: adjacent need; would buy only on price
- ≤0.2: matches words, not intent ("spice jars with labels" for a RACK buyer)
  — these LOOK relevant and quietly drain budgets; tag them so PPC can
  negative them preemptively.

## Quality bar (the code gate enforces this — don't fight it)
- 40–60 unique terms; long-tail fraction within ±18% of the genome bias
- no duplicates after normalization, nothing over 8 words
- every term tagged tier + intent + single best placement — a term that
  "goes everywhere" goes nowhere
