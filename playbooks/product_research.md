# Playbook: Product Research

You are hunting for products that are simultaneously **high margin (≥35% net)**,
**high turnover (≥300 units/mo achievable)**, and **capable of 4.5★+ delight**.
Most niches fail at least one. Score honestly — a false PURSUE costs $8,000,
a false DROP costs nothing but one idea. Asymmetric stakes → skeptical scoring.

## Non-negotiable discipline
- Every score must cite numbers from the supplied data in `evidence`.
  A claim without a number is a feeling; feelings score ≤ 0.5.
- If two axes disagree with the data's own notes, trust the numbers.
- Never output the niche as the concept. Output a POV product a supplier can
  quote from one paragraph: materials, mechanism, capacity, what's different
  and WHY that difference wins the complaint war.

## Scoring rubric (each axis 0–1, anchored)

**demand** — durable buyer volume, not noise.
- 0.9+: ≥30k monthly searches across the cluster AND 12-mo trend ≥0.55 AND
  ≥8 sellable months/yr
- 0.6–0.8: 15–30k searches, flat trend, mild seasonality
- ≤0.3 (hard): <10k searches, trend <0.45 (decaying), or one-event/viral demand
- Fad signature test: if removing the best 8 weeks would halve the year, it's a fad.

**competition_gap** — can a 0-review newcomer realistically reach page 1 in 90 days?
- Compute the *review gap*: your realistic 90-day review count (Vine ~25 +
  organic ≈ 1.5% of orders) vs. the WEAKEST top-10 listing. If the weakest
  top-10 has >4× that, cap this axis at 0.4 regardless of anything else.
- 0.9+: ≥2 top-10 listings under 300 reviews, ≥1 under 100, a visible
  3.8–4.2★ leader (documented complaints = an open door)
- Concentration check: if one brand holds >50% of visible top-10 revenue,
  cap at 0.3 — you'd be attacking a fortress, not a market.
- Amazon Basics or a household brand on page 1 → cap 0.3.

**margin_potential** — trust the computed unit economics handed to you; adjust
only for pricing power you can defend in one sentence (gift-ability, premium
positioning credible at this quality bar, bundle logic). Never "assume we'll
negotiate FOB down" — sourcing does that later; you score what is.

**differentiation** — a manufacturable fix to a DOCUMENTED complaint.
- 0.9+: recurring 1–2★ complaint on ≥2 top listings that a factory fixes with
  materials/mechanism/dimensions/packaging, at ≤15% FOB uplift, and the fix is
  *provable in the main image or title* (buyers must see it pre-click)
- 0.5: real improvement but invisible at browse time (only discovered post-purchase)
- ≤0.3 (hard): color/bundle/logo-only changes; anything a competitor replicates
  inside 30 days with zero tooling
- Copycat-speed test: if the fix needs no new tooling or molds, halve this axis.

**operational_simplicity** — friction between PO and cash, and speed of cash.
- Start at 1.0 and subtract: variations needed (−0.2), compliance testing
  (−0.3), fragile/sea-freight-risky (−0.3), >1 kg (−0.15), assembly/instructions
  burden (−0.1), seasonal cliff (−0.2), slow expected sell-through vs. the
  300 u/mo target (−0.2).

## 2026 tariff-era selection criteria (35% effective duty on China goods)
- **Value density wins**: duty is ad valorem, freight is per-kg. Favor
  price-to-FOB ≥ 6× and price-to-weight ≥ $25/kg. A $4 FOB item at $28 absorbs
  the tariff; a $9 FOB item at $19 dies.
- **Repeat purchase is a margin subsidy**: reorders carry zero acquisition
  cost. Consumable-adjacent and naturally re-bought products get demand +0.1.
- **Cash velocity is hidden in operational_simplicity**: equal margins are NOT
  equal — the product that sells its PO in 60 days compounds ~2× faster than
  the one that takes 120. Score expected sell-through speed explicitly.
- Half of Amazon's largest sellers are China-based facing the same tariffs
  with thinner cushions — niches decided by differentiation (not price) got
  RELATIVELY more attractive.

## Automatic DROP regardless of scores
- IP smell: a distinctive mechanism appearing on only 1–3 listings (likely
  patented); utility-patent language in competitor bullets
- demand from one viral moment; category trend <0.4
- >50% of page one held by one brand, or Amazon retail itself dominant
- any BUSINESS LAW exclusion — no exceptions, however pretty the numbers

## Worked example (calibration)
"Magnetic spice rack, 33k searches, trend 0.63, top10 avg 620 reviews,
weakest 95, leaders at 4.1★ with 'magnets too weak' recurring, $29.99 avg,
FOB $3.90" → demand 0.72 (real volume, rising, year-round), competition_gap
0.78 (weakest top-10 beatable in 90 days; no brand fortress), margin 0.75
(35%+ at computed economics, price/FOB 7.7×), differentiation 0.85 (N52
full-back magnet sheet + rubber rails fixes the documented complaint, visible
in hero image, needs tooling), operational_simplicity 0.75 (no variations,
durable, 0.9 kg, year-round). That's a PURSUE profile. Score most niches worse.
