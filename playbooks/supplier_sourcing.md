# Playbook: Supplier Sourcing & Negotiation (Alibaba)

Goal: land the differentiated spec at a FOB that clears the margin floor —
from a supplier who can hold quality at reorder scale. The spec IS the
product; a great negotiation on the wrong spec is a fast way to ship the
same product everyone else sells.

## RFQ rules (what separates a pro RFQ from tourist mail)
- Lead with exact specs, never "I'm interested in your product": materials
  with grades (SUS304, N52, ABS vs PC), dimensions with tolerances (±0.5 mm),
  finish, weight, packaging (double-wall carton, unit box art-ready, drop-test
  spec), and compliance needs stated as line items.
- The differentiation requirements are MUSTs with acceptance criteria a QC
  inspector can measure: not "strong magnets" but "combined pull force ≥ 8 kg
  on vertical stainless surface, tested loaded".
- Ask for, in one email: FOB at 500/1000/3000 tiers, sample cost + lead time,
  production lead time at each tier, defect-rate policy, tooling costs and
  WHO OWNS THE MOLD (you want ownership or exclusivity in writing), factory
  or trading company (ask for business license + VAT invoice), export
  experience to US/FBA (carton labeling, FNSKU).
- Ask for photos/video of THIS product family on THEIR line. Trading
  companies forward someone else's photos — reverse-image-check.
- Never reveal target price or order budget in the first message.

## Quote comparison (never negotiate the first number)
Get ≥3 quotes and compare TOTAL LANDED, not FOB: FOB + tooling amortized over
first PO + sample costs + inspection + your duty stack. A $0.20 higher FOB
from a factory that owns export compliance beats a cheap quote that fails
inspection. Suspiciously low quote (>15% under cluster) = spec-swap risk;
probe it by asking them to re-confirm the two most expensive spec lines.

## Negotiation ladder principles
- Every ask pairs with a give: price ↓ ⟷ larger tier commitment, relaxed lead
  time, simplified packaging, 30/70 payment terms, a reorder LOI, consolidated
  colors/SKUs. An ask without a give burns goodwill you'll need at QC time.
- Sequence: (1) anchor with the 3000-tier price for a 1000 order, (2) trade a
  reorder LOI for the tier price, (3) trade deposit size for tooling, (4) LAST
  ask is a free improvement (better carton, printed insert) — cheap for them,
  margin for you.
- Payment terms are worth real money: moving 50/50 → 30/70 on an $8k PO frees
  $1,600 for ~45 extra days ≈ half a Vine program of working capital.
- Target FOB must hold the margin floor even if freight +30% and tariffs stay
  at worst case. Walkaway = the FOB where the floor breaks — computed, not felt.
- MOQ strategy: first order small enough that total cash-in (goods + freight +
  duty + samples + photography + 15% buffer) fits the PO share of the launch
  budget. Paying a 10-15% small-batch premium on order #1 is rational: it's
  insurance against a $6k mistake, and you renegotiate at reorder with leverage.

## Supplier red flags (adapt, never skip)
- price far below cluster (spec-swap or bait)
- trading company posing as factory; refuses license/VAT invoice
- refuses third-party Pre-Shipment Inspection — non-negotiable, walk
- no US/FBA export history (carton/labeling failures burn weeks at check-in)
- sample excellence with vague answers about production QC ("same same")
- pushback on mold ownership/exclusivity for YOUR differentiated tooling

## QC checklist baseline (attach to the PO, not after)
- golden sample sealed, photographed, and referenced by number in the PO
- AQL 2.5 pre-shipment inspection; the differentiation acceptance criteria
  are explicit checkpoints with measurement method stated
- function test mirroring the #1 complaint you're fixing (e.g. loaded pull
  test), drop test 80 cm on corners/edges/faces, humidity check for sea freight
- FNSKU labels, carton specs and weights per Amazon FBA requirements verified
  BEFORE the balance payment — the balance is your last leverage; never pay it
  before the inspection report passes
