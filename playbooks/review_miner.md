# Playbook: Review Mining

Reviews are free R&D from the exact buyers you want, written at the moment of
strongest emotion. Mine them into two outputs: manufacturable spec changes
(→ sourcing) and listing copy in the customer's literal voice (→ listings).
Everything else is commentary.

## Method
- Weight by information value: 1–2★ heaviest (what breaks trust and triggers
  returns), then 3★ (the disappointed-but-fair middle — often the most
  specific), then 5★ (the delight vocabulary that becomes copy). Ignore
  1★ logistics-only rants (late delivery) — that's FBA's problem, not spec.
- A theme requires ≥2 independent mentions OR one severe failure mode
  (safety, damage-on-arrival, total function failure). One angry outlier
  is not a theme.
- Estimate theme share: "weak magnets" in 4 of 10 sampled negatives ≈ a
  dominant failure mode worth FOB money to fix; 1 of 10 is a footnote.
  State the share in `frequency` reasoning.
- Translate complaints into FACTORY language with acceptance criteria:
  "magnets too weak" → "N52 full-back magnet sheet, combined pull ≥8 kg
  loaded, verified at PSI". A fix that can't be a QC checkpoint isn't a fix.
- Kano-sort the fixes: **must-be** (absence causes 1★ — fix first, at almost
  any FOB cost), **performance** (more is linearly better — fix if ≤15% FOB
  uplift), **delighter** (unexpected joy — cheap ones only: an insert, a hook,
  better unboxing). Order spec_upgrades accordingly.
- Harvest delight phrasing VERBATIM ("held strong even fully loaded",
  "slowed my golden from 30 seconds to 6 minutes") — copy that mirrors review
  language converts because buyers recognize their own words.

## Classify root cause before prescribing
- Product flaw → spec upgrade (costs FOB)
- Packaging/shipping damage → carton/dunnage spec (costs cents — check first)
- Expectation mismatch ("smaller than expected") → listing fix: dimensions
  diagram, scale-reference photo (costs nothing)
- Instruction gap ("fell because they didn't clean the surface first") →
  insert card + bullet copy (costs nothing, kills a whole 1★ category)
The cheapest category that fully explains the theme wins the prescription.

## Output discipline
- spec_upgrades ordered by (theme share × severity) / cost-to-fix
- every copy_angle traceable to a quoted delight or a preempted complaint —
  no invented benefits: if reviews don't prove it, the listing can't claim it
