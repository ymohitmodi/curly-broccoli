# Playbook: Listing Writer

A listing has two jobs in strict order: (1) get indexed and clicked,
(2) convert the click. Keyword stuffing that hurts readability fails job 2
and therefore — via conversion rate — eventually fails job 1 too. Write for
the buyer; place keywords like furniture, not wallpaper.

Your draft is scored by a deterministic QA checklist (lengths, hooks, policy
phrases, byte limits, keyword front-loading). Failing it triggers a rewrite —
save everyone the round trip and hit it the first time.

## Title (≤200 chars, and the first 80 do all the work)
Formula: [Brand] [Primary Buy Keyword] — [The Differentiator], [Capacity/Size],
[Material/Mechanism], [Use Context]
- The strongest buy-intent keyword AND the differentiator must both appear in
  the first 80 characters — that's the mobile cutoff and what search renders.
- The differentiator is the one that fixes the niche's documented #1 complaint
  ("N52 Magnets, Never Slides") — it's your click-through argument, not decor.
- Title case. No ALL-CAPS words except real specs (N52, BPA). No emojis, no
  "best/top/#1", no promo language, no price. These are policy suppressions
  waiting to happen, and the QA gate rejects them anyway.
- Read it aloud once: if it doesn't parse as a sentence a human would say,
  restructure — word salad titles depress CVR even when they index.

## Bullets (5 × ≤250 chars) — order is strategy, buyers read ≤2
1. **The complaint killer.** Open with the fix to the #1 documented complaint,
   with proof: "NEVER SLIDES: full-back N52 magnet sheet holds 8 kg loaded —
   not the strip magnets that fail in reviews of ordinary racks."
2. **The outcome.** The buyer's life after purchase, in their own review
   vocabulary (review_miner supplies the phrases — use them verbatim).
3. **Fitment/capacity.** Kill the #1 pre-purchase question with numbers
   (fits X of standard Y; dimensions; compatibility). Unanswered fitment
   questions are silent lost sales.
4. **Durability/material proof.** Specifics beat adjectives: "powder-coated
   SUS304" not "premium quality".
5. **What's in the box + support promise.** Reduce perceived risk without the
   words "guarantee/refund/warranty" (policy traps).
- Every bullet opens with a 2–4 word CAPITALIZED HOOK followed by a colon.
- Preempt every risk listed in the task data somewhere in bullets 1–5.

## Description (600–2500 chars)
Story arc: the problem in the buyer's words → agitate with the exact
frustrations from review mining → your fix and why it works mechanically →
spec block → use-case gallery. Short paragraphs; mobile screens. If A+ will
replace it visually, the description still carries indexable long-tail text.

## Backend search terms (≤249 BYTES — bytes, not characters)
Space-separated, lowercase, no commas. ONLY words absent from title/bullets
(repeats are wasted bytes — the QA gate counts them). Spanish variants,
use-contexts, common misspellings if genuinely common. Never competitor
brands or ASINs. Byte-check multibyte characters (ñ, é count as 2).

## A+ outline (5–7 modules, each with a job)
1. Hero lifestyle: product in use, aspirational but honest
2. Differentiator close-up WITH MEASUREMENT: the 8 kg pull test as an image
3. Comparison chart vs "ordinary" products (never named brands) — one column
   per complaint you fixed
4. Use-case grid (kitchen/RV/office) — widens the buyer pool
5. Dimensions/spec diagram — kills returns from wrong expectations
6. Brand story (short) + FAQ from real pre-purchase questions

## Main image brief (the CTR lever — be director-level specific)
Pure white background, product at 85% of frame. The differentiator must be
VISIBLE: loaded to full capacity if capacity is the story; a cutaway of the
magnet sheet if strength is. Specify angle, props, what's in/out of frame,
and the one thing a thumbnail-sized version must still communicate — most
buyers see this image at 200 px, in a row of six identical-looking rivals.
